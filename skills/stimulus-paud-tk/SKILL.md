# Stimulus & Ilustrasi PAUD & TK/RA

Companion skill to asesmen-paud-tk. At the PAUD and TK/RA level a "stimulus" is not a reading passage. It is the concrete medium a child must see, hear, touch, or move with before responding: a large picture, a real object, a song with movements, a picture story told aloud, a puppet, a set of real materials to sort or build with. This skill designs those media for Kelompok Bermain, Taman Kanak-Kanak, and Raudhatul Athfal under Kurikulum Merdeka. There are no long reading texts at this level, and no medium may require the child to read.

This skill is one level of a per-level family. Its same-level siblings are asesmen-paud-tk (the instruments that use the media), modul-ajar-paud-tk (the play planning that requests them), telaah-paud-tk (independent review), and konverter-paud-tk (ready-to-use document formats). Other education levels have their own stimulus skills with written-text calibration; those rules do not apply here.

Note on sources: official documents set no word-count or text-length rules for this level, because assessment media here are oral and concrete. The size, wording, and complexity guidance in the reference file is this skill's practical calibration for young children, stated as practice, not as regulation. The one regulatory anchor is the level's assessment basis itself: for PAUD, formative assessment runs through observation or performance, not written tests (PPA Edisi Revisi 2025, p. 45).

## When this skill applies

- The user asks for a prompt medium for young children: a big picture, a picture-story sequence, a song or movement routine, a real-object set, or a puppet dialogue for an observation or play activity.
- asesmen-paud-tk or modul-ajar-paud-tk needs the media built before instruments or play plans can be finished.
- An existing medium fails: children respond without looking at it, or it is too small, too busy, or too text-heavy to work with this age group.

## The functionality test, early-childhood form

Every medium must pass one test before it ships: cover it up, put it away, or stop the song. If the child can still give the expected response just as well, the medium fails and gets rebuilt or cut.

- A functional picture holds what the child must notice, find, compare, or talk about: the one different object, the sequence that must be ordered, the detail that answers the teacher's question.
- A functional real-object set lets the target behaviour happen: enough pieces to sort, build, match, or share, in the variety the indicator needs.
- A functional song or story carries the content the response depends on: the order of events, the names, the movements that show understanding.
- Name the key objects before delivery: the specific details in the picture, pieces in the set, or moments in the story that the child's response depends on. If you cannot name them, the medium is decoration.

## Two modes of use

Standalone mode: the user asks only for a medium. Deliver the medium (or its full specification), its key objects list, and a one-line note on how the teacher presents it, then stop.

Invoked mode: asesmen-paud-tk or modul-ajar-paud-tk calls this skill with an indicator or play goal. The output is a medium pitched so the target behaviour can actually appear: an indicator about sharing needs a set with fewer attractive pieces than children in the group; an indicator about sequencing needs a picture story whose order is not guessable from a single frame.

## Media types at a glance

- Real objects: sorting, matching, pouring, planting, sharing. First choice whenever safe.
- Large picture cards and picture-story sequences: naming, finding, sequencing, retelling, when the real thing cannot be present.
- Songs, rhymes, and movement routines: sequence, memory, and coordination, observed in what the child sings, does, and keeps in order.
- Puppets and role-play props: language and social behaviour, especially for children who speak more freely through a character.
- Felt-board and magnetic sets: arranging and re-arranging while retelling, so the teacher can watch the child's thinking change.

Full guidance per type, including set composition and quantities, is in the reference file.

## Calibrate to the child

Use the calibration guidance in the reference file as practical defaults: spoken language of a few words per sentence, one idea at a time, vocabulary from the child's daily life, pictures large and uncluttered with one clear focus, and real objects preferred over pictures whenever the real thing is safe and available. Indonesian contexts close to the child: home, school yard, market, garden, rain, animals, family activities.

## Produce media in this order

1. Real objects and real experiences first. A real fruit to peel and share, real leaves to sort, water and containers to pour. Nothing printed beats the real thing at this age when it is safe.
2. Large printed or drawn visuals when the real thing is unavailable, unsafe, or out of season: big picture cards, a picture-story sequence, a felt-board set. Draw or render them large, with thick outlines and clear contrast, or curate an open-licensed image (Wikimedia Commons) and print it big; cite the source under the visual.
3. A [SPESIFIKASI MEDIA] box when the medium is a physical set or scene that must be assembled: the exact format is in the reference file. It states the medium type, the pieces and quantities, the key objects, the safety requirements, and the function the response depends on.

Whichever method is used, the medium ships with its presentation note: what the teacher shows, says, or sings, in what order, and what the children are invited to do with it.

## Safety and suitability rules

- Real objects must be safe for the age group: no sharp edges, no small parts for the youngest children, no toxic plants or materials, washable where mouths may touch them.
- Pictures and stories carry no frightening, violent, or shaming content, and no stereotypes about groups of people.
- Songs and movements must be physically doable by the whole group, including children with limited mobility, with an adapted movement offered where needed.
- Meaning never depends on colour alone; a child who cannot distinguish the colours must still be able to respond.

## Gotchas

- Decoration fails. A cheerful border or a mascot picture that the response does not need is cut, however cute it is.
- Text fails at this level. A medium whose function depends on children reading words is the wrong medium; rebuild it as picture, object, or oral story.
- Small and busy fails. A detailed poster read from two metres away measures eyesight and luck, not the indicator.
- Do not ship a specification with vague quantities. "Some leaves" is not a set; name the pieces, sizes, and counts the behaviour needs.
- Do not present the calibration numbers as official rules. They are this skill's practical defaults for young children, and the honest answer about their source is exactly that.
