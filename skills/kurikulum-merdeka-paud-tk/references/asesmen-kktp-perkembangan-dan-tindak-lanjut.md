# KKTP Perkembangan dan Tindak Lanjut PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 3, Asesmen): how KKTP works at the early-childhood level, the BB, MB, BSH, BSB interpretation categories, and the follow-up stimulation matrix.

Verification status: the KKTP framing follows the PPA Edisi Revisi 2025 (the four approaches on p. 48; criteria as descriptive sentences rather than numbers on p. 64) and was checked against that document. The category descriptors and stimulation matrix are this skill's working elaboration for PAUD, not quotations.

## 1. KKTP at this level: description, not numbers

The PPA names four KKTP approaches (p. 48): deskripsi kriteria, rubrik, skala atau interval nilai, and persentase. For PAUD and TK/RA this skill uses only the first, deskripsi kriteria, because the PPA's own principle is that a criterion is a sentence describing mastery of the goal, not a number (p. 64), and young children's development is reported descriptively.

So a KKTP statement here reads like: "Anak menunjukkan perilaku berbagi alat bermain secara mandiri dalam berbagai situasi bermain", not like a score band. Numeric interval bands and percentages of indicators are tools of the higher levels and are not used in this skill.

## 2. The four developmental categories

Interpret accumulated observation and portfolio evidence per indicator:

- BB (Belum Berkembang): the behaviour has not yet appeared, even when the teacher models it or helps directly.
- MB (Mulai Berkembang): the behaviour appears sometimes, or appears with modelling, reminders, or help, and is not yet consistent.
- BSH (Berkembang Sesuai Harapan): the behaviour appears consistently and independently, matching what is expected for the age group.
- BSB (Berkembang Sangat Baik): the behaviour appears consistently and independently, and the child extends it: applying it in a new play situation, combining it with other skills, or helping peers do it.

Rules for judging:

- Judge per indicator, not per child in general. A child is routinely BSH on one indicator and MB on another.
- Require evidence from several occasions and more than one play context before assigning BSH or BSB.
- Write, for each indicator, the evidence sentence that separates adjacent categories: what "with help" looks like versus "independently", in that indicator's own terms. Generic category definitions alone do not make consistent judgements possible.

## 3. Follow-up stimulation matrix

Follow-up at this level is a change in play stimulation, not remedial teaching:

- BB: offer the play experience again in a more inviting form: richer modelling by the teacher, a peer model, simpler or more attractive materials, smaller steps, and a shorter activity span. Observe again across the next meetings.
- MB: repeat the play with gradually reduced support: model once, then let the child try; vary the materials so the behaviour is practised in more than one context.
- BSH: continue the planned progression; introduce the next goal's play experiences.
- BSB: extend the play: open-ended materials, more complex variations, and roles that let the child help peers, such as demonstrating a game or leading a song.

Differentiation runs through content (materials), process (amount of modelling and support), and product (what the child is invited to make or show), while the goal itself stays the same for the group.

## 4. Reporting to parents

- The semester report is a narrative per element: the category reached, one or two concrete evidence examples from observation or portfolio, and the stimulation planned next.
- Report language stays positive and specific. Describe what the child does now and what is emerging; never attach a fixed label to the child.
- Where development stays well behind expectations across repeated stimulations, the report and the teacher conversation with parents may suggest consulting a professional. That suggestion is care, not a diagnosis, and the documents must not phrase it as one.
