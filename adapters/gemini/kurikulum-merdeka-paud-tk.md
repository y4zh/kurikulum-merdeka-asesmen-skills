# Kurikulum Merdeka PAUD & TK/RA

One skill for Indonesian PAUD and TK/RA only: Kelompok Bermain (KB), Taman Kanak-Kanak, and Raudhatul Athfal under Kurikulum Merdeka (Kemendikdasmen). It holds five roles in a single file so that every task stays inside this level's rules:

1. Play-based teaching modules (modul ajar bermain-belajar).
2. Concrete stimulus media.
3. Observation-based assessment instruments.
4. Independent review of those instruments.
5. Conversion of finished instruments into ready-to-use documents.

Other education levels have their own separate single skills; do not borrow their rules into this one.

Three level hallmarks govern everything below:

- **No written tests at this level.** No test items, no options and keys, no scored worksheets. Assessment runs through observation, performance, play, conversation, and portfolio.
- **Development is reported as BB, MB, BSH, and BSB.** The categories describe development; they are never converted into numbers, percentages, or rankings.
- **Documents only, never test exports.** Finished instruments become print and document formats. No CBT, GIFT, Moodle, or item-bank export exists at this level.

## Cara memakai skill ini

Route the task to one section, read that section together with its reference files, and work only from them. The roles chain in order: a module plans goals and observation points (1), media are built for the play (2), instruments are designed (3), reviewed independently (4), and only then converted into documents (5).

## Catatan status verifikasi sumber

The framework claims used here were checked against the primary documents, above all the PPA Edisi Revisi 2025: formative assessment is not high-stakes and never decides report grades, promotion, or graduation (p. 44); for PAUD, formative assessment runs through observation or performance, not written tests (p. 45); KKTP as descriptive criteria rather than a number follows p. 64, and the descriptive-criteria approach is one of the four approaches named on p. 48. The three-stage experience model (Memahami, Mengaplikasi, Merefleksi) follows the Naskah Akademik Pembelajaran Mendalam (Tabel 3.1, pp. 44-45) and is used at this level loosely, as a play rhythm, not as a cognitive ladder. Process assessment follows Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026: technique and instrument stay aligned with the learning goal (Pasal 8), and teacher self-reflection happens at least once per semester (Pasal 15-19). The CP citation (SK Kepala BSKAP No. 046/H/KR/2025) has not been independently verified; check it in the official document before relying on it in a formal school document. Anything marked a practice rule or working standard below is this skill's design choice, not a regulation quote: the instrument formats, the media calibration numbers, and the review card have no official template, and official documents set no word-count rules for this level because its media are oral and concrete.

## 1. Modul Ajar (bermain-belajar)

A modul ajar at this level is a play plan, not a lesson script: it turns the PAUD Capaian Pembelajaran (CP) elements into play goals, maps play activities across centres and areas, shapes the three deep-learning experiences as forms of play, and places the observation points that Bagian 3 will use. There are no worksheets-as-tests and no written exercises in this section. Background detail lives in [references/modul-ajar-kerangka-bermain-belajar.md](references/modul-ajar-kerangka-bermain-belajar.md).

Applies when:

- The user asks for a modul ajar, a weekly or daily play plan, or a teaching scenario for a KB, TK, or RA class.
- The user needs the CP elements broken down into play goals for a theme or a semester.
- The user wants activities organised by sentra or area, with differentiation for children at different developmental stages.
- The user has observation results grouped by BB, MB, BSH, BSB and needs them turned into next meetings' play stimulations.
- The user asks for teacher reflection instruments for a play-based unit or a semester.

It does not design the observation instruments (Bagian 3), review them (Bagian 4), or format documents (Bagian 5).

Workflow:

1. Read the CP. Identify which of the three PAUD elements the theme serves: Nilai Agama dan Budi Pekerti; Jati Diri; Dasar Literasi, Matematika, Sains, Teknologi, Rekayasa, dan Seni. If the user has not supplied the CP text or at least the element, theme, and age group, ask before planning. Do not invent CP wording and present it as official text; label a paraphrase as a paraphrase.
2. Derive play goals. Break the element into goals a teacher can watch happening in play: one behaviour focus per goal, stated positively ("anak menyebutkan nama teman saat mengajak bermain"), usually two to four goals per theme sequence.
3. Sequence the goals across meetings from supported to independent: first with teacher modelling, then with light support, then freely in play. The sequence is the early-childhood form of an ATP; keep it as a simple ordered list with the support level noted per step.
4. Build the play map. Place activities in the centres or areas the school uses, for example sentra balok, sentra main peran, sentra bahan alam, sentra seni, sentra persiapan, and outdoor play. Each activity names the goal it serves, the materials, the teacher's role, and what children will do.
5. Shape the three experiences as play. Memahami: children meet a phenomenon, story, or material and connect it to what they know, through talk, looking, and trying. Mengaplikasi: children use the idea in play: building it, role-playing it, sorting, cooking, planting. Merefleksi: children retell, show, or talk about what they made and did, with the teacher's questions helping them notice what changed. Never force a written reflection product at this level.
6. Place observation points. For each goal, state which play moments give the teacher a clear view of the behaviour, and what evidence to record. The instruments themselves are built in Bagian 3; here, state what evidence the observation must produce. Technique and instrument stay aligned with the goal (Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026, Pasal 8).
7. Plan differentiation by developmental stage. For each main activity note the supported entry (teacher modelling, a peer model, simpler materials, fewer steps) and the extension (open-ended materials, an added challenge, a helper or leader role). The play goal stays shared; materials, modelling, and support are what differ.
8. Hand off. Close the plan with the clean goal list and the observation points, formatted so Bagian 3 can build instruments directly from them.

Output structure of a modul ajar, in order:

1. Identitas: school (placeholder when unknown), kelompok usia (KB, TK A, or TK B), theme and subtheme, semester, time allocation, number of meetings.
2. CP element, quoted or clearly labelled as paraphrased, with the source the user supplied.
3. Play goals: numbered, one observable behaviour focus each, with the support sequence across meetings.
4. Play map: activities per centre or area, each with materials, teacher role, children's activity, and the goal served.
5. Learning experiences per meeting in the three stages, written as play, with the evidence each stage produces.
6. Lembar kegiatan bermain plan: the play activity sheets or material sets that replace worksheets at this level, with their supported and extended variants.
7. Observation points: placement per goal and the evidence expected, as the handoff to Bagian 3.
8. Reflection: the teacher reflection set from the reference file, trimmed to the unit when the user wants a short version, serving the process assessment of Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026, Pasal 15-19.
9. Handoff block: the goal list and observation points for Bagian 3.

From observation categories to play scenarios. When observation results come back grouped by category, turn each category into a scenario the teacher can run at the next meetings, naming the activity, materials, grouping, and what to observe:

- BB (Belum Berkembang): re-offer the play with full modelling. The teacher plays alongside, demonstrates the behaviour, and keeps the activity short and attractive. Exit evidence: the behaviour appears at least with direct modelling.
- MB (Mulai Berkembang): repeat the play with fading support: model once, then step back; pair the child with a peer who already shows the behaviour. Exit evidence: the behaviour appears with only a reminder.
- BSH (Berkembang Sesuai Harapan): no re-teaching. The child moves on to the next goal's play experiences at the standard level.
- BSB (Berkembang Sangat Baik): extend the play: open-ended materials, a more complex variation, or a role helping peers, such as demonstrating a game or leading a song. Helping roles count as extension only when the child must show and explain, not merely repeat.

These scenarios change the stimulation, never a grade: at this level there are no remedial grades, and observation results never rank children.

Gotchas:

- Do not put test items or scored worksheets in a modul ajar for this level; a plan that drills children for a written check contradicts the level's assessment basis (PPA Edisi Revisi 2025, p. 45).
- Do not copy a CP from memory and present it as the official text.
- Do not force the SOLO ladder onto play. A four-year-old reflects by retelling and showing, not by writing an evaluation.
- Do not list all 8 Dimensi Profil Lulusan as targets for every theme. Name the one to three the play genuinely develops, with the activity as evidence.
- Do not skip the Merefleksi moment to save time. Shorten an activity before cutting it.

## 2. Stimulus & Media

At this level a "stimulus" is not a reading passage. It is the concrete medium a child must see, hear, touch, or move with before responding: a large picture, a real object, a song with movements, a picture story told aloud, a puppet, a set of real materials to sort or build with. There are no long reading texts at this level, and no medium may require the child to read.

Applies when:

- The user asks for a prompt medium for young children: a big picture, a picture-story sequence, a song or movement routine, a real-object set, or a puppet dialogue.
- Bagian 1 or Bagian 3 needs the media built before a play plan or an instrument can be finished.
- An existing medium fails: children respond without looking at it, or it is too small, too busy, or too text-heavy for this age group.

The functionality test, early-childhood form. Every medium must pass one test before it ships: cover it up, put it away, or stop the song. If the child can still give the expected response just as well, the medium fails and gets rebuilt or cut.

- A functional picture holds what the child must notice, find, compare, or talk about: the one different object, the sequence that must be ordered, the detail that answers the teacher's question.
- A functional real-object set lets the target behaviour happen: enough pieces to sort, build, match, or share, in the variety the indicator needs.
- A functional song or story carries the content the response depends on: the order of events, the names, the movements that show understanding.
- Name the key objects before delivery: the specific details in the picture, pieces in the set, or moments in the story that the child's response depends on. If you cannot name them, the medium is decoration.

Two modes of use:

- Standalone mode: the user asks only for a medium. Deliver the medium (or its full specification), its key objects list, and a one-line note on how the teacher presents it, then stop.
- Invoked mode: Bagian 1 or Bagian 3 calls with an indicator or play goal. Pitch the medium so the target behaviour can actually appear: an indicator about sharing needs a set with fewer attractive pieces than children in the group; an indicator about sequencing needs a picture story whose order is not guessable from a single frame.

Media types at a glance: real objects (sorting, matching, pouring, planting, sharing; first choice whenever safe); large picture cards and picture-story sequences (naming, finding, sequencing, retelling, when the real thing cannot be present); songs, rhymes, and movement routines (sequence, memory, coordination); puppets and role-play props (language and social behaviour, especially for children who speak more freely through a character); felt-board and magnetic sets (arranging and re-arranging while retelling). Full guidance per type, including set composition and quantities, is in [references/stimulus-panduan-media-dan-visual-paud.md](references/stimulus-panduan-media-dan-visual-paud.md).

Calibrate to the child. Use the reference file's calibration guidance as practical defaults: spoken language of a few words per sentence, one idea at a time, vocabulary from the child's daily life, pictures large and uncluttered with one clear focus, and real objects preferred over pictures whenever the real thing is safe and available. Indonesian contexts close to the child: home, school yard, market, garden, rain, animals, family activities.

Produce media in this order:

1. Real objects and real experiences first. A real fruit to peel and share, real leaves to sort, water and containers to pour. Nothing printed beats the real thing at this age when it is safe.
2. Large printed or drawn visuals when the real thing is unavailable, unsafe, or out of season: big picture cards, a picture-story sequence, a felt-board set. Draw or render them large, with thick outlines and clear contrast, or curate an open-licensed image (Wikimedia Commons) and print it big; cite the source under the visual.
3. A [SPESIFIKASI MEDIA] box when the medium is a physical set or scene that must be assembled, in the exact format of the reference file: medium type, pieces and quantities, key objects, safety requirements, and the function the response depends on.

Whichever method is used, the medium ships with its presentation note: what the teacher shows, says, or sings, in what order, and what the children are invited to do with it.

Safety and suitability rules:

- Real objects must be safe for the age group: no sharp edges, no small parts for the youngest children, no toxic plants or materials, washable where mouths may touch them.
- Pictures and stories carry no frightening, violent, or shaming content, and no stereotypes about groups of people.
- Songs and movements must be physically doable by the whole group, including children with limited mobility, with an adapted movement offered where needed.
- Meaning never depends on colour alone; a child who cannot distinguish the colours must still be able to respond.

Gotchas:

- Decoration fails. A cheerful border or a mascot picture that the response does not need is cut, however cute it is.
- Text fails at this level. A medium whose function depends on children reading words is the wrong medium; rebuild it as picture, object, or oral story.
- Small and busy fails. A detailed poster read from two metres away measures eyesight and luck, not the indicator.
- Do not ship a specification with vague quantities. "Some leaves" is not a set; name the pieces, sizes, and counts the behaviour needs.
- Do not present the calibration numbers as official rules. They are this skill's practical defaults for young children.

## 3. Asesmen

This section designs the assessment package: observation instruments, not questions. It produces identity, goals derived from the PAUD CP elements, observation checklists, anecdotal record formats, developmental rating scales, performance rubrics, an interpretation guide in BB, MB, BSH, and BSB, a play-based follow-up plan, and a quality audit.

Applies when the user asks for an assessment instrument or package for KB, TK, or RA children (roughly ages 2-6); needs observation checklists, anecdotal records, developmental rating scales, performance rubrics, or portfolio assessment formats; or needs observation results interpreted into BB, MB, BSH, or BSB categories, or a follow-up stimulation plan based on them. It does not apply to written tests, worksheets scored as tests, or any item format with options and keys.

Step 1: Clarification gate. If the request is brief, stop and ask before designing any instrument:

1. Kelompok usia: KB (about 2-4 years), TK A (about 4-5 years), or TK B (about 5-6 years).
2. Elemen CP: which element of the PAUD CP the assessment serves (see Step 2).
3. Tema and subtema: the play theme the observation is embedded in.
4. Teknik: observasi, unjuk kerja, permainan, percakapan, portofolio, or a combination.
5. Purpose: initial developmental mapping, ongoing formative observation, or material for the semester development report.

Skip the gate when the request already answers these points; then state the assumptions at the top of the package.

Step 2: The PAUD framework. Goals come from the Capaian Pembelajaran PAUD, organised in three elements:

1. Nilai Agama dan Budi Pekerti: the child recognises and practises religious values, honesty, helpfulness, politeness, and care for the environment.
2. Jati Diri: the child knows their own identity, manages emotions, builds social skills, and develops gross and fine motor skills and healthy living habits.
3. Dasar Literasi, Matematika, Sains, Teknologi, Rekayasa, dan Seni: the child builds early literacy and numeracy foundations, curiosity about natural and social phenomena, and creative expression.

Derive each assessment goal from one element, written as observable child behaviour: what the child does, says, builds, or chooses during play. A goal that cannot be watched happening ("anak memahami persahabatan") cannot be assessed at this level; rewrite it until the behaviour is visible ("anak mengajak teman bergabung dalam permainan tanpa disuruh").

Assessment purposes at this level:

- Pemetaan awal (diagnostic): maps each child's starting development before a new theme. Assessment at the start of the school year is optional; the PPA emphasises assessment before a new topic or unit begins (PPA Edisi Revisi 2025, p. 44).
- Formatif: ongoing observation during play that guides the next play stimulation. Not high-stakes; it never feeds a numeric grade (PPA, p. 44).
- Laporan perkembangan: the semester narrative report to parents, built from accumulated observation and portfolio evidence, expressed in the BB, MB, BSH, BSB categories.

The technique and instrument chosen must stay aligned with the learning goal (Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026, Pasal 8).

Step 3: Techniques and instruments. Detail and construction rules live in [references/asesmen-teknik-dan-instrumen-observasi.md](references/asesmen-teknik-dan-instrumen-observasi.md). The techniques: observasi with a ceklist (a short list of observable indicators per goal, ticked during play; one indicator, one behaviour); catatan anekdot (short factual notes on significant moments, what the child did and said, without interpretation mixed in); skala rating perkembangan (graded steps per indicator written as behaviour descriptions, not numbers alone); unjuk kerja (the child shows or makes something: retelling with picture cards, building, sorting, singing, role play, scored with a simple descriptor rubric); permainan (structured play situations designed so the target behaviour naturally appears); percakapan (brief, warm questioning during play, recording the child's own words); portofolio (dated work samples and photos of constructions, filed per element, as evidence behind the report categories). Construction rules: indicators are positive and observable; instruments must be usable in real time by a teacher who is also supervising play, so keep each checklist short; every instrument states the play context in which the observation happens.

Step 4: Interpretation with BB, MB, BSH, BSB. KKTP at this level takes the PPA's descriptive-criteria approach: the criterion is a sentence describing the child's development, not a number (PPA Edisi Revisi 2025, p. 64). Interpret evidence into four categories:

- BB (Belum Berkembang): the behaviour has not appeared yet, even with modelling and support.
- MB (Mulai Berkembang): the behaviour appears inconsistently, or only with modelling, reminders, or help.
- BSH (Berkembang Sesuai Harapan): the behaviour appears consistently and independently, as expected for the age group.
- BSB (Berkembang Sangat Baik): the behaviour appears consistently, independently, and the child extends it, for example by helping peers or applying it in a new play situation.

Judge from accumulated evidence across several occasions and contexts, never from a single moment. State, per indicator, what evidence would move a child from one category to the next; that sentence is what makes the categories usable instead of decorative. The full follow-up matrix is in [references/asesmen-kktp-perkembangan-dan-tindak-lanjut.md](references/asesmen-kktp-perkembangan-dan-tindak-lanjut.md).

Step 5: Follow-up is stimulation, not remedial. There is no formal remedial at this level. BB and MB: repeat the play experience with more modelling, smaller steps, simpler materials, or a more inviting play setting; observe again in the following meetings. BSH: continue with the planned play progression to the next goal. BSB: offer extended play challenges: richer materials, leadership roles in group play, or open-ended variations. If a child's development stays far behind across many observations and stimulations, the follow-up is communication with the parents and, where the school judges it needed, a suggestion to consult a professional. The instrument package notes this path; it never labels the child.

Step 6: Output package. Deliver these parts in order: (1) Identitas Asesmen: school (placeholder when unknown), kelompok usia, element and goal, theme, technique, observation period. (2) Tujuan dan Indikator: the goals derived from the CP element, each with its observable indicators. (3) Instrumen: the checklists, anecdotal record formats, rating scales, or performance rubrics, ready to use. (4) Pedoman Pelaksanaan: when and in which play situations to observe, and how to record without interrupting play. (5) Pedoman Interpretasi: the BB, MB, BSH, BSB descriptors per indicator and the evidence rule for moving between categories. (6) Rencana Tindak Lanjut Stimulasi: per category, the next play stimulations to offer. (7) Catatan Telaah Mutu: the self-audit from Step 7.

Step 7: Mandatory self-audit. Run before delivering, and print the result as part 7:

- Alignment and observability: every indicator traces back to a stated goal and one CP element, and names behaviour a teacher can see or hear; rewrite any indicator that names an inner state.
- Language and safety: positive wording, no labels attached to children, standard Indonesian for the written parts; play situations physically and emotionally safe, with no frightening, shaming, or competitive-elimination activities.
- Level purity and media function: no written test, no scored worksheet, and no answer key has slipped into the package; any picture, object, or song used as a prompt must actually be needed for the child to respond (decorative media fail; see Bagian 2).

Gotchas:

- Do not convert BB, MB, BSH, BSB into numbers or percentages for ranking children, and do not assess from a single observation: one moment is an anecdote, not a category.
- Do not write indicators a teacher cannot observe while supervising play; an instrument that needs the teacher's full attention on paper fails in a real classroom.
- Do not present practice rules as regulation. The verified anchors are the PPA passages cited above; the instrument formats themselves are this skill's working designs.

## 4. Telaah Instrumen

This section reviews early-childhood assessment instruments that already exist: observation checklists, anecdotal record formats, developmental rating scales, and performance rubrics. It does not design new instruments; Bagian 3 does that. This section acts as the independent reviewer: it checks each instrument against the review card in [references/telaah-kartu-telaah-instrumen-paud.md](references/telaah-kartu-telaah-instrumen-paud.md) and gives it a verdict with reasons a teacher can act on. Read that file before reviewing a set.

There is no quantitative item analysis in this section, deliberately. This level has no test items and no answer data, so difficulty and discrimination indices have nothing to attach to. Quality here is judged qualitatively: alignment, observability, language, usability, and safety. Anyone offering statistical validation for an observation checklist is selling a number without a basis.

Applies when the user hands over existing PAUD or TK/RA observation instruments and asks for a review, a telaah, a validation, or a quality check; when an instrument set produced with Bagian 3 needs its second pass before classroom use or adoption by a school or teacher working group; or when a teacher wants a second opinion on observation formats from another source. It does not apply to designing instruments from scratch or setting developmental goals; route those to Bagian 3 and Bagian 1.

The independence rule. An author reviewing their own instruments in the same pass will miss their own blind spots; that is why this review exists separately from the Bagian 3 self-audit.

- The self-audit in Bagian 3 is the author's quick check before delivery. This section is the second, deeper pass, run as if the reviewer did not write the instruments.
- Never present a self-audit as if it were this independent review. Name which one a report is.
- Review the instrument as printed, not as intended. If the goal, the CP element, or the interpretation guide is missing, that absence is itself a finding; do not reconstruct what the author probably meant and pass the instrument on that basis.
- When reviewer and author disagree, the card decides: cite the code that fails (for example O2 or K1) instead of arguing taste.

Workflow:

Step 1: Set up the review. Collect what the verdict will be judged against: the instruments, the goal and CP element each indicator serves, the age group, the play context, and the interpretation guide (the BB, MB, BSH, BSB descriptors) where one exists. An instrument that arrives without its goal can still be reviewed for language, usability, and safety, but its alignment verdict is "cannot be confirmed" until the goal is supplied. Say so in the report instead of guessing.

Step 2: Qualitative review with the card. Work instrument by instrument, indicator by indicator, through the card in the reference file:

- Materi codes (M): alignment with the CP element and goal, developmental fit for the age group.
- Observasi codes (O): whether each indicator names behaviour a teacher can actually see or hear during play, one behaviour per indicator.
- Konstruksi codes (K): whether the format is usable in real time while supervising play, and whether its categories and scales are defined.
- Bahasa codes (B): positive, non-labelling language, standard Indonesian.
- Keamanan dan Etika codes (A): physical and emotional safety of the observed activities, and confidentiality of the records.

Record a pass or fail per code, with a short note on any fail that points at the exact indicator or line that causes it. A fail without a location is not actionable.

Step 3: Verdict per instrument. Give every instrument exactly one verdict:

- Terima (accept): every applicable code passes. The instrument may be used as is.
- Revisi (revise): the instrument targets the right goal but has a fixable defect: an indicator naming an inner state instead of behaviour, two behaviours fused in one row, an undefined scale step, a checklist too long to use while supervising. State the fix and supply the revised text in Step 4 or in the output.
- Tolak (reject): the instrument cannot be repaired by editing: it targets no identifiable goal or element, it requires children to read or write, it grades children numerically or ranks them, or its activity is unsafe. State which defect is fatal.

No set-level verdict replaces the per-instrument verdicts. A set can be mostly sound and still contain one instrument that must not be used.

Step 4: Indicator surgery. Run this on every indicator that fails the Observasi codes. This procedure is a working standard of this skill:

1. Diagnose the failure and name it: inner state ("anak memahami...", "anak menyukai..."), fused behaviours (two or three actions in one row), trait language ("anak rajin"), or context-free wording that could mean anything in any activity.
2. Name the observable behaviour the indicator was reaching for: what the child does, says, builds, chooses, or shows, in the stated play context.
3. Rewrite the indicator as that behaviour, in positive wording, one behaviour per row, with the play context named where the behaviour only makes sense in one.
4. Re-check the repaired set: every indicator observable, the set still covers the goal, and the interpretation guide's category descriptors still match the rewritten indicators.

Show the before and after text for every operated indicator. Never change the goal an indicator serves during surgery without flagging it as a goal change and re-running the Materi codes.

Output format. Deliver the review in this order:

1. Ringkasan Telaah: how many instruments were reviewed, by type, and the count per verdict (terima, revisi, tolak), plus any set-level problem such as a missing interpretation guide.
2. Tabel Putusan per Instrumen: one row per instrument with its name, type, verdict, the card codes that failed, and a one-line reason.
3. Teks Revisi: the full revised text of every instrument marked revisi, including operated indicators shown before and after.
4. Catatan Kategori: whether the BB, MB, BSH, BSB descriptors are defined per indicator and usable, since an instrument without them cannot feed a development report.

Write reasons in plain language a teacher can act on without opening the reference file. Codes support the reason; they do not replace it.

Gotchas:

- Do not invent observation data to "validate" an instrument statistically. No items, no indices; the card is the instrument of judgement here.
- Do not pass an instrument because its intention was good. Review what is printed.
- Do not rewrite a whole instrument during indicator surgery. Surgery touches indicators; a wrong goal or an unsafe activity is a revise or reject verdict with the reason stated.
- Do not let numeric scoring slip back in through the review: a checklist that totals ticks into a grade fails the level's basis, however tidy the format looks.
- Do not treat the review card as verified law. Its content has not been independently checked against official Pusmendik documents; it is this skill's working review instrument, built for this level's instrument types.

## 5. Konverter Dokumen

This section turns a finished instrument set into the documents teachers actually hold and file: printable observation sheets, anecdotal record formats, portfolio checklists, and the narrative child development report format. Conversion is a packaging job, not an editing job: indicators, categories, and wording come from the source set and leave unchanged. If something in the source is broken or missing, this section reports it back instead of repairing it silently.

There is no digital test export in this section, explicitly: no CBT file, no GIFT, no Moodle XML, no CSV item bank. Those formats exist to deliver test items to a computer, and this level has no test items and no computer-delivered assessment. Format details and templates live in [references/konverter-format-dokumen-paud.md](references/konverter-format-dokumen-paud.md).

5. For a filled report (not just the format): the accumulated observation records and portfolio notes per child. A report narrative is written from evidence the user supplies; this section never invents a child's development.

Outputs. Produce only the documents the user asked for:

- Lembar Observasi Cetak: one sheet per play session or per week, with the identity header, the play context line, the indicator rows, the observation columns, and the notes space, laid out to be filled by hand while supervising play.
- Format Catatan Anekdot: a record format (one per page or a running log) with separated fields for the factual record and the later interpretation, plus the indicator link.
- Ceklist Portofolio: one checklist per child per element, listing the evidence entries to collect across the semester, with date and teacher-note columns.
- Format Laporan Perkembangan Anak: the semester report shell and, when evidence is supplied, the filled narrative: per element, the category reached (BB, MB, BSH, or BSB), the evidence in plain parent-readable language, and the stimulation planned next.

Layout rules for every document are in the reference file: large handwriting space, few items per page, and headers that survive photocopying.

Conversion rules:

- Indicators and descriptors are copied from the source set unchanged. A converter that rewords an indicator breaks the audit trail between the reviewed set and the document in the teacher's hand.
- Categories stay categories. Never convert BB, MB, BSH, BSB into numbers, percentages, letter grades, or a class ranking anywhere in a converted document, including in totals rows or summary tables.
- One source, many documents: generate every requested document from the same source set in the same pass, so the observation sheet, the portfolio checklist, and the report format can never drift into different versions of an indicator.
- A filled narrative reports only supplied evidence. Where evidence for an element is thin, the narrative says the observation is still ongoing; it does not fill the gap with generic praise.

Pre-conversion validation checklist. Run this pass before writing any output document, and report the result with the delivery:

- Every instrument in the set names its goal and CP element; documents are not produced for instruments that lack them.
- The interpretation guide exists and its category descriptors match the indicators in the set, indicator by indicator.
- Indicator wording is identical across all documents being produced in this pass.
- Identity data is complete for the headers; no placeholder such as [Nama Sekolah] remains in a document meant for direct use. Placeholders are acceptable only in a blank template the user explicitly asked for.
- The roster, when per-child documents are requested, is the roster the user supplied, complete and unaltered.
- No document contains a score column, a total, or a ranking field. Their presence means a higher-level format has leaked into this level's documents; remove and report.

Any failure stops the conversion for the affected documents. List the failures and hand them back; do not guess a descriptor, drop an indicator quietly, or invent identity data.

Gotchas:

- Never invent observation results. An empty report shell is a valid deliverable; a filled report without supplied evidence is fabrication.
- Keep records confidential in form as well as content: per-child documents are produced per child, not as one class sheet that exposes every child's categories to whoever holds the page, unless the user explicitly asks for a teacher-only class overview.
- Handwriting space is a functional requirement, not decoration. A beautiful sheet with rows too small to write in fails in the classroom exactly like a wrong indicator does.
- Do not offer a spreadsheet of categories as a "summary with scores". A teacher-only overview may list categories per child for planning; it still carries no numbers and no ranking.

# KKTP Perkembangan dan Tindak Lanjut PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 3, Asesmen): how KKTP works at the early-childhood level, the BB, MB, BSH, BSB interpretation categories, and the follow-up stimulation matrix.

Verification status: the KKTP framing follows the PPA Edisi Revisi 2025 (the four approaches on p. 48; criteria as descriptive sentences rather than numbers on p. 64) and was checked against that document. The category descriptors and stimulation matrix are this skill's working elaboration for PAUD, not quotations.

## 1. KKTP at this level: description, not numbers

The PPA names four KKTP approaches (p. 48): deskripsi kriteria, rubrik, skala atau interval nilai, and persentase. For PAUD and TK/RA this skill uses only the first, deskripsi kriteria, because the PPA's own principle is that a criterion is a sentence describing mastery of the goal, not a number (p. 64), and young children's development is reported descriptively.

So a KKTP statement here reads like: "Anak menunjukkan perilaku berbagi alat bermain secara mandiri dalam berbagai situasi bermain", not like a score band. Numeric interval bands and percentages of indicators are tools of the higher levels and are not used in this skill.

## 2. The four developmental categories

Interpret accumulated observation and portfolio evidence per indicator:

- BB (Belum Berkembang): the behaviour has not yet appeared, even when the teacher models it or helps directly.
- MB (Mulai Berkembang): the behaviour appears sometimes, or appears with modelling, reminders, or help, and is not yet consistent.
- BSH (Berkembang Sesuai Harapan): the behaviour appears consistently and independently, matching what is expected for the age group.
- BSB (Berkembang Sangat Baik): the behaviour appears consistently and independently, and the child extends it: applying it in a new play situation, combining it with other skills, or helping peers do it.

Rules for judging:

- Judge per indicator, not per child in general. A child is routinely BSH on one indicator and MB on another.
- Require evidence from several occasions and more than one play context before assigning BSH or BSB.
- Write, for each indicator, the evidence sentence that separates adjacent categories: what "with help" looks like versus "independently", in that indicator's own terms. Generic category definitions alone do not make consistent judgements possible.

## 3. Follow-up stimulation matrix

Follow-up at this level is a change in play stimulation, not remedial teaching:

- BB: offer the play experience again in a more inviting form: richer modelling by the teacher, a peer model, simpler or more attractive materials, smaller steps, and a shorter activity span. Observe again across the next meetings.
- MB: repeat the play with gradually reduced support: model once, then let the child try; vary the materials so the behaviour is practised in more than one context.
- BSH: continue the planned progression; introduce the next goal's play experiences.
- BSB: extend the play: open-ended materials, more complex variations, and roles that let the child help peers, such as demonstrating a game or leading a song.

Differentiation runs through content (materials), process (amount of modelling and support), and product (what the child is invited to make or show), while the goal itself stays the same for the group.

## 4. Reporting to parents

- The semester report is a narrative per element: the category reached, one or two concrete evidence examples from observation or portfolio, and the stimulation planned next.
- Report language stays positive and specific. Describe what the child does now and what is emerging; never attach a fixed label to the child.
- Where development stays well behind expectations across repeated stimulations, the report and the teacher conversation with parents may suggest consulting a professional. That suggestion is care, not a diagnosis, and the documents must not phrase it as one.

# Teknik dan Instrumen Observasi PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 3, Asesmen): the regulatory anchors for early-childhood assessment, the purposes assessment serves at this level, and construction rules for each observation instrument.

Verification status: the PPA claims below were checked against the PPA Edisi Revisi 2025 and carry page citations. The instrument formats themselves are working designs of this skill, not official forms.

## 1. Regulatory anchors

- Panduan Pembelajaran dan Asesmen (PPA), Edisi Revisi 2025: the framework document for assessment at every level, including PAUD.
- Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026 (Standar Proses), Pasal 8: the assessment technique and instrument must fit the learning goal. At this level that means the technique must fit what a young child can show through play and daily activity.
- The CP for PAUD is organised in three elements: Nilai Agama dan Budi Pekerti; Jati Diri; Dasar Literasi, Matematika, Sains, Teknologi, Rekayasa, dan Seni. Goals and indicators are derived from these elements, not from school subjects.

## 2. Purposes, and what each one may be used for

- Pemetaan awal: maps starting development before a new theme or unit. Assessment at the start of the school year is optional; the emphasis is on assessment before teaching a new topic begins (PPA, p. 44).
- Formatif: ongoing observation during play. It is not high-stakes: it must not be used to decide report grades, promotion, or graduation (PPA, p. 44). For PAUD, formative assessment is carried out through observation or performance, and does not use written tests (PPA, p. 45).
- Laporan perkembangan: the semester report to parents. It is a narrative built from accumulated evidence, expressed in the developmental categories BB, MB, BSH, BSB. It is never a numeric score and never a ranking of children.

## 3. Instrument construction rules

### Ceklist observasi

- One indicator per row, one observable behaviour per indicator.
- Columns: child name, date, indicator, observed or not yet observed, and a short note column. Keep the list short enough to use while supervising play; ten indicators is already a lot for one play session.
- State the play context at the top of the sheet: which centre, area, or activity the observation happens in.

### Catatan anekdot

- Record significant moments factually: date, context, what the child did and said, in the child's own words where speech matters.
- Keep observation and interpretation in separate parts of the note. Interpretation is written later, against the indicators.
- Anecdotes are evidence for category decisions, so file them per child and per element.

### Skala rating perkembangan

- Each scale step is a behaviour description, not a bare number: what the behaviour looks like with full support, with some support, independently, and extended.
- The scale steps should line up with the BB, MB, BSH, BSB interpretation in the companion reference file, so ratings convert into report language without re-judging.

### Rubrik unjuk kerja

- For show-and-make activities: retelling, building, sorting, singing, role play, simple experiments with real objects.
- Rows are aspects of the performance; columns are behaviour descriptors from emerging to extended. Keep aspects few (three to five) so the rubric is usable while watching.

### Portofolio

- Dated work samples and photographs of constructions, filed per child and per element.
- Each entry carries a one-line teacher note naming the indicator it evidences. A portfolio without those notes is a folder, not assessment evidence.

## 4. Recording ethics

- Record behaviour, not character judgements: "menunggu giliran setelah diingatkan dua kali", not "anak tidak sabaran".
- Observation records are confidential working documents for the teacher, the school, and the child's parents.
- Never use observation results to compare or rank children publicly.

# Format Dokumen PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 5, Konverter Dokumen): layout rules and field structures for the four document types this level uses.

Note on sources: these formats are working designs of this skill, not official government forms. Schools with their own mandated report format keep their format; this skill then converts the instrument set into that format's fields instead, and says so in the delivery note.

## 1. Layout rules for all documents

- Handwriting first: rows at least 1 cm high for tick columns and 1.5 cm where notes are written; a teacher fills these standing up, mid-play.
- Few items per page: one observation sheet carries one play session's indicators for one group, never a whole semester squeezed onto a page.
- Headers survive photocopying: identity in plain text at the top, no meaning carried by colour or shading alone.
- Every sheet names its instrument source (goal and CP element) so a filed sheet can be traced back to the set it came from.

## 2. Lembar Observasi: field structure

Header block:

- Satuan Pendidikan
- Kelompok Usia dan Kelas (KB, TK A, or TK B, plus the class name)
- Elemen CP dan Tujuan
- Tema dan Subtema
- Konteks Bermain (centre, area, or activity)
- Tanggal dan Nama Guru

Body: one row per child per indicator, or one row per indicator with a class grid, as the source set specifies. Columns: Nama Anak, Indikator (printed in full), Teramati (tick), Belum Teramati (tick), Catatan Singkat. A closing block holds the teacher's follow-up note for the next meeting's stimulation.

## 3. Format Catatan Anekdot: field structure

One record per significant moment, or a running log with the same fields repeated:

- Nama Anak, Tanggal, Konteks (activity, place, who else was involved)
- Catatan Fakta: what the child did and said, in factual language, the child's own words quoted where they matter
- Tafsiran (diisi kemudian): the indicator this moment evidences, and what it suggests about the child's current category on that indicator
- Tindak Lanjut: the stimulation to offer next

The physical separation of Catatan Fakta and Tafsiran on the page is a requirement, not a style choice: it keeps later judgement from rewriting the record.

## 4. Ceklist Portofolio: field structure

One checklist per child per element, covering one semester:

Header: child name, kelompok usia and class, element, teacher.

Rows: one per evidence entry the plan expects (work sample types, construction photographs, anecdote references), with columns: Jenis Bukti, Tanggal Dikumpulkan, Indikator yang Dibuktikan, Catatan Guru (one line naming what the entry shows). A closing section lists gaps: indicators with no evidence yet, which is the checklist's real purpose mid-semester.

## 5. Format Laporan Perkembangan Anak: structure

The semester report to parents, one per child:

Header: school identity, child name, kelompok usia and class, semester and academic year, teacher.

Body, repeated per CP element (Nilai Agama dan Budi Pekerti; Jati Diri; Dasar Literasi, Matematika, Sains, Teknologi, Rekayasa, dan Seni):

- Kategori Capaian: one of BB (Belum Berkembang), MB (Mulai Berkembang), BSH (Berkembang Sesuai Harapan), BSB (Berkembang Sangat Baik), taken from the interpretation guide's judgement, never averaged across indicators into a score.
- Narasi Perkembangan: a short paragraph in parent-readable language describing what the child does now, with one or two concrete examples from observation or portfolio evidence.
- Stimulasi Lanjutan: what the school will offer next semester, and one simple suggestion for home that matches it.

Closing: a space for the teacher's overall note, the parent's response, and signatures. The report contains no numbers, no class rank, and no comparison with other children, anywhere.

# Kerangka Bermain-Belajar PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 1, Modul Ajar): the graduate profile dimensions as they appear in early childhood, the three-stage experience model read as a play rhythm with its SOLO background, and the reflection instruments.

Verification status: the stage-to-SOLO mapping in section 2 follows the Naskah Akademik Pembelajaran Mendalam, Tabel 3.1 (pp. 44-45), and the process-assessment cycle in section 3 follows Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026, Pasal 15-19; both were checked against the primary documents. The graduate-profile citation (Permendikdasmen No. 13/2025) and the CP citation (SK Kepala BSKAP No. 046/H/KR/2025) have not been independently verified.

## 1. Dimensi Profil Lulusan in early childhood form

The graduate profile has eight dimensions. In PAUD and TK/RA they appear as habits and dispositions practised in play, not as subject outcomes. A modul ajar names only the dimensions its play activities genuinely develop.

1. Keimanan dan Ketakwaan terhadap Tuhan Yang Maha Esa: praying before activities, gratitude, honesty, and care for living things, practised as daily habits.
2. Kewargaan: following shared play rules, respecting friends who are different, taking part in class duties.
3. Penalaran Kritis: asking why, guessing and trying, and noticing what happened, in simple concrete situations.
4. Kreativitas: making, building, drawing, and pretending in ways that are the child's own idea.
5. Kolaborasi: playing together, sharing materials, taking turns, and finishing a group construction.
6. Kemandirian: choosing an activity, tidying up, dressing and eating with growing independence.
7. Kesehatan: washing hands, moving actively, eating well, and playing safely.
8. Komunikasi: telling what they did, listening to a friend, and showing work to the group.

## 2. The three experiences as a play rhythm

Pembelajaran Mendalam runs learning through three experience stages, with three working principles: learning that is mindful, meaningful, and joyful. In early childhood the stages are a rhythm inside a play session or across a theme, not separate lessons:

| Stage | What it looks like in play | SOLO background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memahami | Children meet a story, phenomenon, or new material; the teacher connects it to what they already know through talk and exploration. | Unistruktural moving to Multistruktural: one aspect held, then several. |
| Mengaplikasi | Children use the idea in play: building, role play, sorting, planting, simple cooking. | Relasional: the parts are used together. |
| Merefleksi | Children retell, show, or talk about what they made and did; the teacher's questions help them notice and name what happened. | Berpikir Abstrak yang Mendalam in its earliest, oral and concrete form. This is the Naskah Akademik's term for the fifth SOLO level; "Extended Abstract" is Biggs & Collis's original English term. |

Below the stages sits Prastruktural, which the Naskah Akademik's table carries as the status "Belum Memahami": a child there has not yet entered the experience, and the plan's response is invitation and modelling, not a stage activity.

Use this mapping loosely. It guides the teacher's planning of richer play; it is never a label attached to a child, and no child at this level is asked to produce written reflection.

## 3. Reflection instruments

Reflection is part of process assessment. Permendikdasmen No. 1 Tahun 2026, Pasal 15-19 (verified) requires assessment of the learning process after teaching, at least once per semester, carried out by the teacher through self-reflection (Pasal 15) and also by fellow teachers, the school head, and students (Pasal 16-19). For young children, the student side is oral and pictorial: a closing circle where children show and tell, never a written form.

### Teacher self-reflection

Answer in writing, briefly, with evidence from the play sessions:

1. Play flow: did the Memahami, Mengaplikasi, Merefleksi rhythm actually happen, or did the session stay at free play without the teacher's connecting talk? Which moment showed it?
2. Observation: did the observation points catch children's development early enough to adjust the next meeting's stimulation, and what did I change because of an observation result?
3. Differentiation: did the supported entry reach the children who needed modelling, and did the extensions genuinely stretch the most developed children, or did both only receive the standard activity with a different label?
4. Next cycle: which single element (materials, grouping, centre layout, or time use) will I change first, and what would I expect to observe if the change worked?

### Children's closing reflection (oral)

Short prompts for the closing circle, answered by showing and telling, not graded:

1. What did you make or play today that you like most? Show us.
2. What was hard? Who helped you?
3. What do you want to play or make next time?

# Panduan Media dan Visual PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 2, Stimulus & Media): calibration for young children, media types, curated image sources, and the media specification format.

Note on sources: every figure in this file is a practical default of this skill. Official documents set no word counts, picture sizes, or text lengths for this level, because its assessment media are oral and concrete (the level's assessment basis is observation and performance, PPA Edisi Revisi 2025, p. 45). Never present these defaults as Kemendikdasmen rules.

## 1. Calibration for young children (practical defaults)

| Aspect | KB (about 2-4 years) | TK A (about 4-5 years) | TK B (about 5-6 years) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spoken prompt length | 3-5 words per instruction, one instruction at a time | About 4-7 words, up to two linked instructions | About 5-8 words, a short sequence of instructions |
| Picture story | 3 frames, one event per frame | 3-4 frames | 4-6 frames, a simple cause and result |
| Picture character | One large object per card, plain background | One scene, few objects, one clear focus | One scene with several readable details |
| Real-object sets | Big pieces, 2-3 categories to sort | Pieces sized for small hands, 3-4 categories | Fuller sets, patterns and sequences possible |
| Song and movement | Very short, one repeated movement | Short verse, movements mirror the words | Longer song, movement sequence to remember |

Vocabulary comes from the child's daily life: family, home, school, food, animals, weather, the garden, the market. Introduce at most a few new words per medium, and let the medium itself show what they mean.

## 2. Media types and when to use them

- Real objects (realia): for sorting, matching, counting, pouring, planting, cooking, and care activities. First choice whenever safe and available.
- Large picture cards: for naming, finding, comparing, and talking when the real object is unavailable, unsafe, or out of season. One focus per card, thick outlines, clear contrast, printed large enough to read across the circle.
- Picture-story sequences: for sequencing, retelling, and predicting. The order must not be guessable from one frame alone, or the sequence measures nothing.
- Songs, rhymes, and movement: for memory, sequence, body coordination, and joy. The response the teacher observes is in the words sung, the movement matched, or the order kept.
- Puppets and role-play props: for language and social behaviour. A puppet lets a shy child answer through a character; observe the child's language and choices, not acting talent.
- Felt-board and magnetic sets: for retelling and arranging, where pieces can be moved and re-moved while the child talks.

## 3. Curated sources for real images

When a real photograph is genuinely needed (an animal the school cannot keep, a natural phenomenon), search open sources first and cite the source under the visual every time:

- Wikimedia Commons (commons.wikimedia.org): open-licensed photographs of animals, plants, natural phenomena, and places.
- BMKG (bmkg.go.id): Indonesian weather and sky imagery, when a weather picture must be real.

If no open source supplies a suitable image, draw the picture or write a specification box instead of using an uncredited image from elsewhere.

## 4. [SPESIFIKASI MEDIA] block format

Use this exact block when the medium is a physical set or scene a teacher must assemble. Fill every field; the key objects are what the child's response depends on, and the function line is the functionality test stated in advance.

```text
[SPESIFIKASI MEDIA]
Jenis Media    : Set Benda Nyata / Kartu Gambar Besar / Rangkaian Cerita Bergambar / Lagu dan Gerakan
Isi dan Jumlah : Daun asli 3 bentuk berbeda, masing-masing 6 helai, ukuran minimal sebesar telapak tangan anak; 3 keranjang sortir.
Objek Kunci    : 1) Perbedaan bentuk tepi daun (halus, bergerigi, membulat), 2) Jumlah daun tiap bentuk sama banyak.
Keamanan       : Daun tidak beracun, dicuci sebelum dipakai, tidak ada bagian tajam; benda kecil tidak dipakai untuk kelompok KB.
Penyajian      : Guru menunjukkan satu daun tiap bentuk, menyebut namanya, lalu mengundang anak mengambil dan mengelompokkan daun ke keranjang.
Fungsi         : Anak mengelompokkan berdasarkan bentuk tepi daun; respons tidak mungkin tepat tanpa melihat dan memegang daunnya.
```

## 5. Print and display checks

- Readable across the circle: if the medium is shown to a group, every child must see the key objects from where they sit.
- Contrast and size beat detail: fewer, bigger elements always win at this level.
- Laminate or mount handling materials where possible; media that fall apart in small hands end the activity, not the assessment.
- Check the medium with the functionality test one last time in its final physical form, not only in the draft.

# Reference file: references/telaah-kartu-telaah-instrumen-paud.md

# Kartu Telaah Instrumen PAUD & TK/RA

Reference for the kurikulum_merdeka_paud_tk skill (Bagian 4, Telaah Instrumen): the qualitative review card for early-childhood observation instruments, and the interpretation-guide checks that accompany it.

Note on sources: this card has not been independently verified against official Pusmendik documents, so treat it as a working review instrument, not as a verified quotation of an official form. It is adapted to this level's instrument types: there are no test items here, so the card reviews indicators, formats, and interpretation guides instead of stems, options, and keys. The level's regulatory anchors sit in Bagian 3 (Asesmen) of this skill: formative assessment at PAUD runs through observation or performance, not written tests (PPA Edisi Revisi 2025, p. 45), and is not high-stakes (PPA, p. 44).

## 1. Materi: alignment and developmental fit

- M1: Every indicator traces to a stated goal, and the goal traces to one of the three PAUD CP elements (Nilai Agama dan Budi Pekerti; Jati Diri; Dasar Literasi, Matematika, Sains, Teknologi, Rekayasa, dan Seni).
- M2: The behaviour expected fits the stated age group (KB, TK A, or TK B): neither babyish for the group nor beyond what children of that age can show.
- M3: The instrument's indicator set covers the goal without large gaps and without indicators that belong to a different goal or element.
- M4: The play context named in the instrument is one in which the behaviour can genuinely appear; an indicator about sharing cannot be observed in solitary activity.

## 2. Observasi: is the behaviour actually observable

- O1: Each indicator names behaviour a teacher can see or hear: an action, an utterance, a choice, or a product. Indicators naming inner states ("memahami", "mengerti", "menyadari") without a visible sign fail.
- O2: One behaviour per indicator. Rows that fuse two or more behaviours ("anak menyusun balok dan berbagi dengan teman") fail, because the two halves can develop at different speeds.
- O3: The indicator is stated positively, as the behaviour to be seen, not as the absence of a problem.
- O4: The indicator is specific enough that two teachers watching the same moment would record the same judgement.

## 3. Konstruksi: the format must work in a real classroom

- K1: The instrument is usable in real time by a teacher who is also supervising play: short lists, large enough writing space, no format that needs the teacher seated at a desk.
- K2: Every scale step or category the format uses is defined in behaviour words on or with the instrument; a bare 1-4 scale with no descriptors fails.
- K3: The recording fields are complete for evidence use: child, date, context, and a notes space. Records without dates cannot support a development judgement.
- K4: Anecdotal formats keep the factual record and the interpretation in separate parts.
- K5: Portfolio formats require a dated entry and a teacher note naming the indicator each entry evidences.

## 4. Bahasa: language about children

- B1: Written parts follow standard Indonesian usage (EYD V).
- B2: Language is positive and descriptive. Trait labels attached to children ("pemalu", "nakal", "lambat") fail wherever they appear, in indicators, categories, or example notes.
- B3: Instructions to the teacher are unambiguous: one reasonable reading of what to observe, when, and how to record it.
- B4: No wording that would shame a child or a family if the record were read aloud to the parents.

## 5. Keamanan dan Etika: safety of activity and record

- A1: The observed activities are physically safe for the age group: materials, tools, water, heights, and outdoor settings all checked.
- A2: The activities are emotionally safe: no frightening content, no public comparison between children, no elimination or shaming mechanics.
- A3: The instrument does not require children to read, write, or sit a timed task; any such requirement fails the level's assessment basis outright.
- A4: Records are treated as confidential: the format and its instructions name who may see them (teacher, school, the child's parents) and do not invite public display of individual results.

## 6. Interpretation guide checks

An instrument set that feeds a development report must ship with its interpretation guide. Check:

- The BB, MB, BSH, BSB descriptors are written per indicator, not only as one generic definition for the whole set.
- Adjacent categories are separated by an observable difference (for example "with modelling" versus "independently"), not by adverbs alone ("cukup baik" versus "baik").
